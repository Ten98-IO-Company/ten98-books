# Reports (M1)

The M1 reports of one company: the day book, the trial balance, profit and loss, the balance sheet, and the drill-down from a statement through the group summary, a ledger's monthly summary and its vouchers to a single voucher. Every report is read-only, runs through `withCompany` in read mode (viewers read), reads typed rows from SQL with a statement-timeout budget of its own, and returns amounts as `bigint` minor units on the server and decimal strings on the wire (D-03, D-07, D-09). The contract is [D-01, D-04, D-05, D-09, D-10 and D-11](DECISIONS.md); this page describes what is built.

Code: [src/server/reports/](../src/server/reports) (report modules and CSV tables), [src/server/ledger/reports.ts](../src/server/ledger/reports.ts) (the shared read model: one row per ledger for a period), [src/server/api/reports.ts](../src/server/api/reports.ts) (HTTP), [src/lib/reports.ts](../src/lib/reports.ts) (query schemas, shapes, formatting helpers), screens in `src/routes/app/c.$companyId.reports*.tsx` with their frame in [src/components/reports/](../src/components/reports). Keys are in [KEYBOARD.md](KEYBOARD.md#reports).

## What counts

Only regular, undeleted vouchers affect any balance. Cancelled, deleted and optional vouchers never do. A post-dated voucher counts from its own date like any other. Opening balances are computed at report time (D-04): a balance-sheet ledger opens with its opening balance (as of the books-beginning date) plus everything before the period. An income or expense ledger opens at zero, and everything it gathered before the period's start, earlier years and earlier months of the same year alike, is closed into the profit and loss ledger's row (a loss as a debit, a profit as a credit). An opening balance entered on an income or expense ledger stays on that ledger for a period starting on or before the books begin and is closed into the row for a later one. Opening balances entered out of balance (D-10) are shown as a difference line on the short side, so every statement still agrees.

So a trial balance for February, in a year whose books begin on 1 January, shows each income and expense ledger with February's movement only and one profit and loss row holding January's result; the same trial balance from 1 January has no such row (it is zero) and shows the cumulative ledgers. Both balance. This was observed on the reference workflow in a company without stock and in a second year of a company with stock, where the row held the earlier year and the earlier months together, and in a mid-year ledger monthly summary and ledger vouchers export of an income ledger, which had no opening balance. Until November 2026 ten98-books closed only earlier financial years into the row and kept the same year's earlier months on the income and expense ledgers as opening balances; that is what changed. Profit and loss (a period's movement) and the balance sheet (read from the financial year's start) did not change, and neither did the voucher entry balance preview, which reads from the start of the voucher date's financial year, so an income ledger shows its balance for the year to date while a voucher is entered. The single-ledger balance read (`GET ledgers/<id>/balance?from=&to=`, [LEDGER.md](LEDGER.md)) is the trial balance row, so for a period that starts after the books begin its income and expense figures follow the new rule.

## Periods

- A period is two calendar dates (`YYYY-MM-DD`, in the company's calendar), the start not after the end, at most ten years long. The balance sheet takes one date.
- Statements, the group summary and the ledger screens open on the financial-year start through the **last voucher date**: the latest date of a regular, undeleted voucher that is not post-dated (optional and post-dated vouchers do not move it), or the books-beginning date while there is none. The day book opens on that one day.
- The period is part of every report's address (`?from=&to=`), so a report survives a reload and can be linked. Every report needs both dates in its address; with only one of them (or a start after the end) it opens on its default period and rewrites the address. The one exception is the balance sheet, an "as at" report: `?to=` alone is the as-at date, with the start of that date's financial year as its `from` (`asAtPeriod`), and a `to` that is no date (`2026-02-30`, `soon`, a year outside 1900 to 2199) opens the default date with a notice that names the text it could not read; a lone `from` is ignored there too. Which period a lone date should mean on the other reports is not decided. A statement's period carries from one report to the next, both when drilling down and through "open another report"; a statement opened from the menu without one uses the last statement period shown in the tab, or the default above. The day book's day never becomes the statements' period: the day book opens on its own default day, and "open another report" from the day book or a voucher does not carry its date.
- Profit and loss covers one financial year at most (a longer period is refused with a plain message). The ledger monthly summary shows one financial year at a time: its end is clipped to the end of the year that contains its start. The scope line and the as-of date state the period actually shown, and a notice says what was asked for and why it ends earlier (`monthlyClipNotice`).
- A trial balance or group summary may span several financial years. Everything before the period's start is closed into the profit and loss ledger; income and expense keep accumulating across any year end inside the period, so such a trial balance shows several years' income and expense on their own ledgers. Use one financial year for a period-end trial balance.
- A period or date before the books begin shows the opening balances: the books-beginning entries count as the balance at the start of that day, with no movements before it.

## Reports

| Report | API (`GET /api/companies/<id>/reports/...`) | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Defaults | `defaults` | The last voucher date, the default statement period, the day book's default day, the books-beginning date |
| Day book | `day-book?from=&to=[&limit=][&after=]` | Undeleted vouchers dated in the period, by date and entry order, 200 per page (keyset cursor). Particulars are the first line's ledger, and the voucher amount stands in that line's debit or credit column. Cancelled and optional vouchers are listed and marked but left out of the totals; post-dated vouchers are marked and counted. A stock journal (migration 0013, item lines only) is listed and counted with no particulars and an amount of zero. A voucher created cancelled without lines (a void, migration 0009) is listed once on its date with its number and the cancelled mark, no side and an amount of zero (`particulars` and `side` are null; empty in the CSV); on the screen its row is named by its voucher type, "(cancelled, no lines)", and it opens the voucher view like any row. Totals cover the whole period, not just the page |
| Trial balance | `trial-balance?from=&to=` | The group hierarchy (primary groups by nature, then sub-groups, then ledgers) with opening debit and credit, period debit and credit, closing debit and credit. A group's columns are the sums of its ledgers' debit and credit balances kept apart, so a group can show both. The profit and loss ledger, outside every group, holds the income and expense before the period. The opening difference line and grand totals that agree |
| Profit and loss | `profit-and-loss?from=&to=` | Trading income and cost of sales (groups flagged for gross profit) to the gross profit or loss; other income and other expenses to the net profit or loss. A ledger's figure is its movement in the period, plus its opening balance when the books begin on or after the period's start (inside it, or after it: a period before the books begin shows the opening balances), so the balance sheet's current period and the statement it opens agree |
| Balance sheet | `balance-sheet?date=` | Capital and liabilities, then assets, by group, with closing balances from the start of that date's financial year. The profit and loss account carries the earlier years' result (brought forward: the profit and loss ledger) and this year's result to date (current period); a profit stands with the liabilities, a loss with the assets. The difference line when openings disagree |
| Group summary | `group-summary[/<group id>]?from=&to=` | One group's direct sub-groups and ledgers with opening balance, debit, credit and closing balance; without a group, the primary groups and the ledgers outside every group |
| Ledger monthly summary | `ledger-monthly/<ledger id>?from=&to=` | The ledger's opening balance at the start of the period (zero for an income or expense ledger in a period that starts after the books begin), then debit, credit and closing per calendar month (the first and last months clipped to the period) |
| Ledger vouchers | `ledger-vouchers/<ledger id>?from=&to=[&limit=][&after=]` | One row per voucher touching the ledger in the period: date, particulars (the first ledger on the other side and how many more), type, number, debit, credit and the running balance; opening, period totals and closing. Paged like the day book; the running balance continues across pages |

Register pages read live data. Each page is read when it is shown, from the voucher after the previous page's last one, so a voucher entered meanwhile behind the cursor (dated before it) counts in the running balances and the totals of the later pages but is not listed until the earlier pages are read again (the first page reloaded, or the period changed). Example: six receipts paged three at a time; a receipt entered on day 2 after page 1 was shown makes page 2 start from the higher balance, and the day book's totals count seven vouchers while the two pages list six. Reload the register to see it whole. When a period needs more than one page, the register's summary line names the vouchers the page shows, for example "Vouchers 1 to 200; more on the next page." or "Vouchers 201 to 303, the last of the period." (`registerRange` in `src/lib/reports.ts`); the line is ordinary content, so it prints as well. The numbers are positions in the listing as it was read, so they shift when vouchers are added or removed between page loads; an empty page (an old address past the last voucher) names no range. The period's total number of vouchers is not shown: the server does not count them.

Each report except defaults has a CSV export at the same path plus `/export` (for example `trial-balance/export?from=&to=`), with the same authorization: RFC 4180 records through the runtime CSV helpers, every cell quoted, a cell that could start a spreadsheet formula prefixed with an apostrophe, a UTF-8 byte-order mark so spreadsheets read Arabic names, plain decimal amounts with the currency's decimal places (never a minus sign, which the formula guard would turn into text), balances as an amount column and a Dr or Cr column, statement lines (profit and loss, balance sheet) as an amount column and a side column (the side of the line's section, or the other side for a figure that went the other way, which the screen shows as negative; result lines have no side), and a level column instead of indentation. An export holds the whole period, up to 100,000 lines, and each user may export 20 times a minute (more answers 429 "Too many requests. Wait a minute and try again."); reading reports is not limited.

Amounts in JSON are decimal strings; a signed balance is positive on the debit side and negative on the credit side. Unknown or repeated query parameters are refused (400), as are a group id that names no group and a ledger id that names no ledger (404). A company the user is not a member of answers "not found" (404) for every route and export; a company is a workspace ([TENANCY.md](TENANCY.md)).

### Bills (M2a slice 1)

| Report | API (`GET /api/companies/<id>/reports/...`) | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Bills receivable or payable | `bills-outstanding?side=receivable\|payable[&groupId=]&asOf=[&limit=][&after=]` | Bills pending on the list's side at the date (debit for receivable, credit for payable) of the ledgers below both trade receivables and trade payables, or below the group asked for (`groups` in the answer): bill date, name, party, pending, due date, days overdue, by party and bill name (in the order of the names' match key, compared as plain text, observed; equal keys by bill), 200 a page (`limit` up to 1,000, `after` is the previous page's `next`). With current statistics on `bill`, a page reads about its own bills, at any depth of the list. Without statistics (a new installation), or with statistics older than the company's bills, a page may read up to the company's bills: once to find the parties with bills, plus the party's bills from the cursor. It never reads another company's bills, and never the table (LEDGER.md, "Known limits", has the figures measured on one fixture); the total of every page comes with the first page and is null on later ones. A bill on the other side is in the other list, whatever the party's group (observed: a customer's advance, a credit bill of a debtor, is listed under bills payable and not under bills receivable; a supplier's advance is a receivable). Earlier builds covered each list's own trade group only and left such bills out |
| Party bills | `party-bills/<ledger id>?asOf=` | One party's bills pending on either side (method, dates, pending, overdue), its on-account amount and its balance. On account is computed from the lines (each line less its bill rows, and the opening balance less its opening bills), so bills plus on account equalling the balance is a check |
| Bill detail | `bill/<bill id>?asOf=` | The bill's rows, opening rows first, then by date, voucher, line and row. Rows that count at the date carry the running pending; rows of cancelled, deleted or optional vouchers, and rows dated after the date, stand among them without one, marked by their status. The method shown is the dating row's own for that row and a settlement for every other live row |
| Ageing | `ageing?side=receivable\|payable[&groupId=]&asOf=&basis=bill\|due` | The listed bills per party in the company's ageing columns (`ageing-buckets`, none until defined), always ending with an open-ended column (added after a closed last one, or the only one while none is defined) so the columns add up to the total, by days since the bill date or past the due date; a bill on a column boundary sits in the earlier column (observed: 90 days in "60 to 90"). By due date, bills not yet due have their own column (an assumption until observed) |

A bill's date and due date are those of the row that dates it: its earliest-dated live new reference or advance, else its earliest-dated live settlement. The pending list for entry (`ledgers/<id>/bills`) offers only bills dated on or before the voucher date. Each report has `/export` with the same rules as the M1 exports. Bill lists have the statements' 30-second budget, one party's reports the ledger screens' 20 seconds.

### Cost centres (M2a slice 2)

| Report | API (`GET /api/companies/<id>/reports/...`) | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Cost category summary | `cost-category-summary?from=&to=[&categoryId=]` | Per category (by name), its top-level centres (no parent) with opening, debit, credit and closing; each top-level centre's figures include all its sub-centres at any depth, whatever their own categories, and sub-centres have no row of their own (observed, also after a parent moved to another category: its child, left in the old category, counts in the parent's row and the parent's new category, and the old category shows nothing for it). A top-level centre, or a category, without figures for the period (no opening, debit or credit) has no row (observed). No figure for line amounts left without centres (observed) |
| Cost centre break-up | `cost-centre-breakup/<centre id>?from=&to=` | One centre: its direct sub-centres, whatever their categories (each with its own sub-centres; observed), and the ledgers of its own rows, with opening, debit, credit and closing; the total equals the centre's figures in the summary |

Rows count when their voucher does (regular and undeleted; a post-dated voucher from its own date). Reports group rows by each centre's category as it is now, not the category a row was saved with (observed: a centre moved to another category is regrouped, while its vouchers keep the old one). Every row counts once. The category figures and the break-up total are our own additions: the reference's cost reports have no total row and no unallocated row. Opening (the observed default): rows dated before the period on balance-sheet ledgers carry forward; rows on income and expense ledgers show the period movement only. Each report has `/export` with the same rules as the M1 exports; the summary has the statements' 30-second budget, one centre the ledger screens' 20 seconds. `tests/cost-centres-golden.integration.ts` reproduces the harbourview company's expected summary for 2026 and at its two checkpoints.

Not built yet, all observed: the centre summary under a category (which lists only the sub-centres in that category, so a parent shows its own amount there) and its monthly summary; the break-up's group-wise view (this one lists ledgers); the cost break-up of a ledger and of a group; the "show opening balance" report option (off by default in the reference; with it on, centres of categories used on income and expense ledgers have no opening, and those used on balance-sheet ledgers carry theirs). There is no cost-centre profit and loss in the reference.

**Screens.** `/reports/cost-category-summary` and `/reports/cost-centre-breakup?centre=<id>` (`src/routes/app/c.$companyId.reports_.cost-*.tsx`), on the report frame of every other report: the period, CSV export, print, the drill trail and Escape back, read-only for viewers. The summary lists each category in bold with its top-level centres below it, with opening, debit, credit and closing; Enter on a centre opens its break-up. The break-up names the centre and its category, lists the sub-centres and then the ledgers with the server's total, and Enter on a sub-centre opens its break-up and on a ledger that ledger's vouchers for the period. Opened without a centre (from the menu), the break-up lists the company's centres to choose from; the ledger list (`/reports/ledger`) works the same way. The find field has focus when the page opens, typing narrows the list, and Enter opens the highlighted line only once the list was narrowed or the highlight moved (arrows, a click, or coming back from a line); with nothing chosen it says so instead of opening the first line (`pickerEnter`). Keys: [KEYBOARD.md](KEYBOARD.md#reports). When the company has cost centres switched off, both screens show the masters screen's notice and still show the saved amounts, which keep counting. Keys: [KEYBOARD.md](KEYBOARD.md#reports). Two drill-downs differ from the reference, which opens a centre summary from the category summary and a ledger's monthly summary restricted to the centre from the break-up: the centre summary is not built, and **the ledger vouchers report has no centre filter** (the server offers none), so a ledger opened from a break-up shows all its vouchers for the period, every centre's and unallocated ones alike, and its totals are the ledger's, not the centre's. Both screens say so: the break-up's line under the title, and a notice on the ledger vouchers opened from it (`fromCentre` in the address, so a reload and the voucher view and back keep it; without a trail, Escape there goes back to that break-up). A centre filter on the ledger reports is a server change still to come.

### Stock (M2b slice a)

Item lines (slice b1, [LEDGER.md](LEDGER.md) "Item lines") move quantities only. A posted item line moves the item's quantity (readable from slice b2) and, on an invoice, posts its derived ledger line, which posts like any ledger line; it does not change the "Opening stock" row, profit and loss opening or closing stock, the balance sheet's closing stock or the opening difference. No cost of goods is posted, and a stock journal posts nothing to the accounts. So every statement below balances as before.

A company that holds opening stock (any `stock_opening` row, [LEDGER.md](LEDGER.md) "Stock masters") gets stock lines in the statements. Until valuation arrives (slice c) the stock value at every date is the opening rows' value, so the lines below are frozen at it and no statement's profit changes.

- **Trial balance.** An "Opening stock" row (`kind: 'stock'`) first inside the seeded current assets group (by `system_key`), on the debit side in the opening and closing columns, with no period movement, counted in the group's and the grand totals. The opening difference counts it: entered ledger openings plus the opening rows' value, so a company whose ledger openings fall short by exactly its opening stock shows no difference. For a period that starts after the books begin, the profit and loss row carries earlier income and expense as before; the carried stock change it will also carry is zero until valuation. The row has no drill-down. The verification read `GET trial-balance` answers `openingStock` beside its ledger rows. The group summary of current assets lists the row too.
- **Profit and loss.** "Opening stock" first and "Closing stock" last in the cost of sales (closing stock as a negative amount, against the section), both at the opening rows' value; the answer carries `stock: { opening, closing }`. Gross and net profit are unchanged.
- **Balance sheet.** "Closing stock" first in current assets at the same value; the answer carries `closingStock`. Both sides agree with stock in them. A loss stays a positive line on the assets side, so each side's total carries it, as observed on the reference's balance sheet of a company with stock and a loss (both sides 521,297.25 for the sunrise demo, where valuation will move 53,382.95 from the loss line into closing stock, on the same side, so its total stays).
- A company without opening rows has none of these lines (`stock` and `closingStock` are null, `openingStock` is null).
- Verified by `tests/stock-statements.integration.ts` on a fixture of its own (full years, February of the first year and February of the second) and by `tests/demo-large.integration.ts` on the seeded demo companies: sunrise holds 37,270.00 of opening stock and gulfline 369,440.00, each with no opening difference in either year; sunrise's profit is the excluding-stock figures of its expected.json (gross -92,681.50, net -341,322.50). Its balance sheet balances at 521,297.25 a side, expected.json's total (not yet compared by `demo:compare`: slice c).
- **Not built.** Valuation (average cost and FIFO per location), the stock summary and the stock reports, the stock change in later periods' profit and loss row, and `demo:compare`'s stock lines (slice c). On the trial balance and group summary screens the stock row is not openable, and the profit and loss and balance sheet screens open nothing for a line without a target.

## Drill-down and screens

A report that opens on its default period says so in its scope line, for example "1 January 2027 to 27 February 2027 (the financial year to the last voucher date)", because the company home states the financial year of today's date, which can differ (`defaultScopeNote`). A refused request (status 400: a period too long, an end before the start) shows the server's sentence and offers Change period, the same command as the period key, first; a failure that may pass on a retry (no connection, a server error) offers Try again (`reportFailureAction`). The badge above the sentence still reads "Report could not be refreshed": it is the design system's wording for any failed load.

Enter goes down one level and Escape goes back one level, to the line it left from (D-05, D-09: drill-down is by route):

- trial balance → group summary (group line) or monthly summary (ledger line). The profit and loss ledger's row opens that ledger's monthly summary, whose opening balance is the result carried in from before the period; the reference does not open this row. We keep it open because the screen it leads to explains the figure and nothing is lost by it
- profit and loss → group summary or monthly summary
- balance sheet → group summary, monthly summary, or the profit and loss statement (the profit and loss account and current-period lines)
- group summary → a sub-group's summary or a ledger's monthly summary
- monthly summary → that month's ledger vouchers. The month is the vouchers' period, so for an income or expense ledger a month after the first opens at zero and ends on that month's movement, not on the summary's running closing. The profit and loss ledger is the other side of the same rule: a month after the first opens with the income and expense result carried up to that month (for example a month whose summary line closes at zero, from the year start, shows January's result in its vouchers' opening and closing). We keep the month a period of its own rather than giving the summary a per-month carried figure, because the summary is one period and its months add up to it, as every other ledger's do
- ledger vouchers and day book → the voucher, read-only
- cost category summary → a centre's break-up; break-up → a sub-centre's break-up, or a ledger's vouchers (not filtered by the centre, see above)

A register's page turn replaces the history entry instead of adding one, so the browser's Back from page 2 leaves the register (as a period change does). That is deliberate: a page turn is the same level (`reportLevel`), Escape goes one level up and not one page back, and a go-to return belongs to one history entry (`heldReturn` in `src/lib/commands.ts`), which a pushed page would leave behind. The way to the previous page is the Previous button, or ArrowUp or PageUp past the first line.

Going back follows the path taken in this tab and restores the highlighted line (`hl` in the address of the level left) and, on a register (day book, ledger vouchers), its page: the cursors of the pages before it are in the address too (`pages`), so a reload or a link keeps the page as well. A level stays on the path when its period, page or view changes after the drill, and its highlight is kept when a period change clears it from the address. A level reached any other way (a link, a reload) goes back to its logical parent: a group to its parent group or its statement, a ledger to its group, a month's vouchers to the monthly summary, a voucher to its day.

Every level adds up to the one above it: a statement line equals its group summary's total, a group summary row its monthly summary's total, a month's debits and credits its vouchers' (and its closing too, except for the later months of an income or expense ledger and of the profit and loss ledger, above). The integration tests walk the whole golden fixture to show it; `tests/reports-period.integration.ts` covers periods that start after the books begin (the February case, a second year, a period across a year end, openings on income ledgers, books beginning inside the year, three decimals, the drill-downs, the statements, the balance preview and the export), and `tests/reports-property.integration.ts` checks random vouchers against sums over the posted lines.

Screens show amounts right-aligned with grouping and the company's decimal places (2 or 3), debit and credit in their own columns and balances as "amount Dr" or "amount Cr". The statements have a condensed view (primary groups) and a detailed view (every sub-group and ledger), lines without amounts hidden unless asked for, and the totals that must agree side by side with whether they do. Tables have a caption, row headers and the design system's focus ring; the layout uses logical directions and holds in a right-to-left document. Print goes through the browser, with the design system's print styles (the app adds none): the sidebar, the top bar, the action bar, buttons, view switches and the skip link are left out and colours print light, so a report prints its summary line, title, scope (the period), source (company and currency), as-of date and status note, and then its table with the totals; a statement, the trial balance and the other one-page reports print every line, a register (day book, ledger vouchers) only the page on screen, whose summary line then names the vouchers shown; the highlighted line keeps a thin bar at its start. An open dialog is left out of the printout.

## Performance

Measured on a generated company of 52,784 voucher lines (12,600 four-line vouchers over two years, 42 ledgers) as `books_app` with row-level security, PostgreSQL 18: the trial balance query took 46.7 ms for two years and 39.6 ms for half a year; a 200-row page of one ledger's vouchers 18.2 ms; the day book page 3.5 ms and its period totals 37.8 ms. Over HTTP against the development server every report answered in 39 to 135 ms. Budgets: statements 30 s, ledger screens 20 s, day book 15 s.

## Not in M1

- Stock valuation: stock stands at its opening rows' value in every period (see "Stock"); no inventory columns in the day book.
- Cost centre reports beyond the two above, foreign-currency bills, interest on bills, multi-currency columns, tax reports, budgets; bill reports have no screens yet.
- Comparative and additional columns (previous period, other periods, percentages), monthly views of a group, a cash-flow or funds-flow statement, ratio analysis.
- Configurable layouts (vertical or horizontal statements, narrations in the day book, filters, saved views); the day book filter by voucher type.
- Editing from a report: the voucher view is read-only.
- Printing a whole register: a printed day book or ledger vouchers holds only the page on screen (up to 200 vouchers); the other pages are printed one at a time, or the whole period exported as CSV.
- Print output was checked by applying the print styles to the trial balance and the day book on screen in Chromium, not in a print preview or on paper; page breaks, the repeated table header and the page width are unverified. The company appears in the source line only, not as a heading.
- Table paging keys and virtualization: the table handles Up, Down, PageUp and PageDown (ten lines), Home, End and Enter on its focused line, and on a register turns the page when a key moves past the first or last line (`onPageEdge`); the report commands do the same with focus elsewhere on the page ([KEYBOARD.md](KEYBOARD.md#reports) has the one difference). Tables render every row: long registers are bounded by the API's 200-row pages and statements render every group and ledger; the table's `virtualize` option is not used.

Requirement status follows D-01: none of this is described as equivalent to any other product.
